There’s a cat I see sometimes. Well, not see. It’s more like he appears. Behind the old butcher shop, near the old, abandoned house. Always when it’s damp out.

He’s not what you’d call clean or tame. The left ear’s half gone, fur like he rolled under a truck and just kept going. Some nights I think I’m imagining him. But then he looks at me, and I know I’m not that creative. He looks like he don’t give a goddamn about your rules, your clocks, your seven lives.

Seven’s plenty. Seven’s a curse.

Charles Bukowski once wrote,

> And a cat never knows fear — finally — he only winds up into the spring of the sea and the rock, and even in a death-fight he does not think of anything except the majesty of darkness.

I think if I ever could leave this skin behind, the job, the phone charger, the name, I’d want something like that. Not a restart, not a second chance. Just quiet.

A way to exist without anyone needing anything from you.

We wouldn’t be friends, he and I. He’d walk, and I’d walk a little ways behind. Maybe we’d sit by a pipe and watch rats. Perhaps we’d share one, or not.

Mornings would happen, I guess, and nights too. We wouldn’t say anything about it. We’d sleep wherever the ground didn’t argue. Once, I dreamt we slept inside a rusted car frame. He made a circle. I curled up near the door.

I wouldn’t need much.

Someone once told me cats don’t know about love. They know. They know it’s a wound that doesn’t heal. I’ve had one press its head against mine and not move for a long time. That meant something or it didn’t. It stayed with me either way.

When the seventh life claws its way out of me, the last breath leaves me in a ditch, ribs rising once, twice. I hope he finds me. He might sniff, blink once, maybe he’ll sit and wait until the flies come, maybe not.

He’s got places to be, probably. He won’t say goodbye.

And that’ll be okay.
