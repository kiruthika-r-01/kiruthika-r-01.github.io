I found out on Instagram. Opening Insta has become like opening the fridge at home for me, I don't want anything in particular, I just open it and doomscroll in between my work (ik, I'm working on it). This afternoon CSK posted that MS Dhoni has retired as a player and will be the team's mentor from IPL 2027. The owner and the co-owner said nice things about him in the statement. Dhoni himself didn't say anything. Of course he didn't.

One thing first. I've never been the obsessed fan who wants to meet their idol up close. I take lessons from them, stand at a distance and support them, and I'm pretty sure I picked up that habit from him too, which is a strange thing to learn from a man I've mostly seen on a TV screen.

I grew up in a small town where cricket was the only sport everyone knew, apart from that one nerd group who loved football. My mom watched every match. I started sitting with her and my cousin when I was around ten, and I knew exactly two things: four and six. I didn't even know how many balls were there in an over. So I'd sit there during the most nail-biting overs and torture them with questions, like why can't they just hit a six every ball if six is the best thing? I got scolded. A lot. I can't blame them either, the match would be going down to the last over and I'd be asking something like that. But that's how I picked up the game, from my mom and my cousin, slowly, with scoldings.

The day India won the 2011 World Cup, I didn't really understand what had happened. I only knew everybody was happy, at home and at school. Our school van driver gave all of us chocolates because India won. Much later I found out Dhoni had finished that final with a six, which is funny to me now, because he's the same man who'd teach me why you don't hit a six every ball.

Bowling was my first love. I learnt the yorker by watching Bhuvi on TV and then trying it out with my neighbour anna, and yes, I said learnt, not mastered. I can still bowl one decently though. Batting came later, and that was because of Suresh Raina and MS. On match days I used to run from school just to switch on the TV in time. Then I did to my cousins exactly what my mom did to me. I made three of them sit and watch with me until they admired Dhoni as much as I did, and all three of them still do.

I really started admiring him in the RPS days, which were hard days to be his fan. CSK got suspended from the IPL for two seasons, 2016 and 2017, and Dhoni went to play for Rising Pune Supergiants. They were bad that first year and finished seventh out of eight. Their last game of 2016 was against Kings XI Punjab in Visakhapatnam, both teams already out, nothing left to play for except not finishing last. Pune needed 23 off the last over. Axar Patel was bowling. Dhoni hit three sixes and a four in that over, and the last ball went for six. 64 not out off 32 balls. Monstrous.

Then the management dropped him as captain. In February 2017 the owners made Steve Smith the captain and told the media Dhoni hadn't stepped down, they just wanted someone young. He had already given up the India captaincy on his own a few weeks before that. So now he was only the wicketkeeper batter, and he never made any fuss about it. In April that season Pune were chasing 177 against Sunrisers Hyderabad and needed 47 off the last three overs, and he made 61 not out off 34 and hit the last ball for four. I think that's the match I started loving him for real, as a player with no armband who still wanted the last over more than anybody else on the ground. Pune made it to the final that year and lost it to Mumbai by one run.

What I love most is how he handles pressure. India and CSK played so many playoffs and finals with him, and in those games all the pressure sits on the captain. He'd stay calm, but you could tell he was thinking about every single ball, almost obsessively. I learnt that from him. On bad days at work I try to do the same, keep my face calm and keep thinking.

He was patient long before anybody knew his name. Before India, he worked as a ticket collector at Kharagpur railway station for about three years, and in his first ever match for India, in December 2004 against Bangladesh, he got run out on the first ball he faced.

There's one thing I hated about him though. He was too conservative. He took risks by being conservative, if that makes sense, waiting and waiting and trusting that he'd finish it in the last over. It worked most of the time. Not every time. From his bad side I'd learn to adapt quickly and be a little more progressive.

The 2019 World Cup semi-final against New Zealand is where it didn't work. On the first day, 9 July, I was at school. It rained in Manchester with New Zealand on 211 for 5, and the match got pushed to the reserve day. For the reserve day I deliberately took leave. The match would be in the second half of the day, but my school was from 8.30 AM to 5.30 PM and I'd only reach home by 6.30, and I was scared I'd miss everything. My dad scolded me. Even my mom wanted me to go to school. I stayed home anyway. India lost wickets early, and Dhoni did what he always does, he took it deep, 50 off 72 balls with just one six. Then he went for the second run, Martin Guptill hit the stumps directly from the deep, and that was it. I cried. It's one of the most heartbreaking moments of my life, and part of me still thinks his waiting game cost us that day.

He got run out on the first ball of his India career and he got run out in his last innings for India. Oh, how I wished he'd get a proper send-off after that, like the one his team gave Sachin in 2011, carrying him around Wankhede on their shoulders.

He didn't get one. On 15 August 2020, Independence Day, I was at home doing my college assignments (copying my friends', I'm a corona batch student, hmph) and he put up an Instagram post. Old photos from his career, a Mukesh song, Main Pal Do Pal Ka Shayar Hoon, playing behind them, and a caption that said "from 1929 hrs consider me as Retired." That was the farewell. Raina announced his retirement the same evening, a little after him. The two people who made me love batting, gone in one evening.

I got to watch him live only once. It was a weird time. CSK's social media team would randomly post that the gates were open for a practice match, and I was lucky I lived near Chepauk then. The day I saw one of those posts, I ran to the stadium. He hit a six, his helicopter shot, and that was my first and last time watching him play live. I stayed in the stands, that was close enough. I did buy a CSK jersey with 07 on it a year back, but I'm still not an obsessed fan. Just saying.

Now he's done with the IPL too, and again it's just a post. He's 45, and the injuries have been eating into his seasons for a while. He'll be in the dugout as a mentor, still in yellow. I'm happy for him, I am. It still hurts a bit, in that odd way it hurts when someone you've never met changes jobs. But hey, he'll always be my captain.

One day I'll tell my kids stories about cricket, and MS will definitely be in them. I'll tell them about the RPS days and that last over in Vizag. And one of them will probably interrupt me to ask why everyone doesn't just hit a six every ball, and I'll scold them a little, because that's tradition.
