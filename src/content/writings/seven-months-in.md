As I sit down to reflect on my journey, I can’t help but feel a mix of emotions — confusions, anxieties, but also a light at the end of the tunnel.

I remember those early days at my first job, a wide-eyed rookie exploring the unfamiliar grounds of the SaaS marketing arena.

But beneath the surface, a feeling of restlessness stirred. The questions kept coming, relentless and insistent. Is this what I truly want? Am I on the right path? The answers remained hard-to-catch, surrounded by a fog of uncertainty.

Seven months into my first job in the SaaS marketing field, I made a decision, one that would totally shake up my work and personal life. I decided to quit my job. It was born out of a deep desire to explore the marketing field but I was stressed and confused about what to do and how to move forward. I had too many choices going around in my mind, like planning to do an MBA or trying my luck on the development side (as I’m a BCA graduate and I had wanted to be a developer long ago). But I never followed through on any of those things because I was too scared to take those big leaps. And mainly because I felt guilty about asking my parents for money.

The aftermath is a roller coster of emotions — stress, anxiety, confusions, and the paralyzing fear of unknown. I wrestled with the classic question: what’s next? The possibilities seemed endless yet overwhelming. The fear of failure, of disappointing those who had faith on me, kept me paralyzed.

So, I started applying again for content writer and product marketer roles. I was so desperate to get into the SaaS field specifically.

And that’s when I got into featureOS.

At the final part of my interview process, our founder, Karthik Kamalakkanan, said something that completely changed my perspective on believing in someone. He said, ‘In 2 years, I see you as someone who can handle a team at featureOS.’ This statement shook me to the core because no one had ever expressed such belief in me before. My self-doubt was overwhelming at that time, but the team at featureOS helped me overcome it within the first month. I am immensely grateful to our founder for believing in me so deeply.

When I first joined featureOS, I was a nervous wreck, with tons of insecurities. Imposter Syndrome had taken hold of me and convincing me I didn’t belong. I mean, who was I to think I could make it in this fast-paced SaaS arena. I was just an introverted, inexperienced kid with a head full of doubts and insecurities.

But from the day one, the energy just felt..different. I was welcomed with open arms. They asked for my ideas, valued my input. For the first time, I felt seen, heard, like I truly belonged.

And that’s the thing about featureOS — they don’t just talk the talk when it comes to inclusion and empowerment, they walk the walk. Every single day.

The culture is one of relentless positivity and growth. Mistakes aren’t met with criticism; they believed in the trial-and-error-grow policy with a lot of compassion. For a rookie like me, I felt so blessed to work with my manager and the team at featureOS.

When I first entered my first job, I used to eat and drink coffee alone. But, when I joined featureOS, the coffee breaks and team lunches felt different. During those coffee conversations, I would definitely learn something new from my team.

With each challenge and milestone, I could feel myself grow stronger surer, more confident in who I was and what I was capable of.

All those years, it wasn’t that I couldn’t do it, but that I didn’t believe in myself. At featureOS, I discovered way more than just a way to pay the bills. I found something that lit a fire in my soul and made me realize just how valuable I truly am.

So when I said goodbye after those life-changing 7 months, I wasn’t just walking away from a job. I was stepping into a brand new chapter of my life as a completely different person. Someone who knows, deep down in her bones, that she can tackle anything that comes her way, no matter how big or scary it seems.

If there’s one thing I learned, it’s this: when you find a place that sees your potential, even when you can’t yet see it yourself, and gives you the tools and the courage to step into your greatness? You don’t walk, you run towards it. And you sure as hell never look back.
